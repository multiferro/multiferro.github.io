Sergey Lisenkov

Assistant Research Professor, Department of Physics, University of South Florida

I study ferroelectric, antiferroelectric and multiferroic materials with first-principles and atomistic simulations: how their polarization, structure and response to fields change with temperature, strain, pressure and size, and how those changes can be turned into useful properties such as solid-state cooling, piezoelectricity or electrical control of spin.

Before coming to USF in 2008 I was a postdoctoral researcher at the University of Arkansas. I received my Ph.D. in physics in 2005 from the Institute of Biochemical Physics of the Russian Academy of Sciences in Moscow and an M.S. from Volgograd State University.

Research

Read more about each topic
  • Antiferroelectrics at small scale

    What happens to PbZrO3 and its relatives when a film is only a few unit cells thick.

  • Hybrid and halide perovskites

    Ferroelectric semiconductors whose spin splitting can be switched with a field.

  • Caloric effects

    Electrocaloric, elastocaloric and multicaloric cooling from direct atomistic simulations.

  • Relaxors and disorder

    How chemical disorder reshapes phase transitions in Ba(Ti,Zr)O3 and BaTiO3.

  • Multiferroics

    Coupled polarization and magnetism in BiFeO3 under fields and strain.

  • Methods and low-dimensional materials

    Effective Hamiltonians, molecular dynamics and machine learning built on first-principles data.
